# Hailing Jin — Dimension 03: Scientific Reasoning DNA

## Scope, identity and evidence boundary

This is a neutral reconstruction of recurring scientific reasoning in identity-audited Hailing Jin team publications, checked against independent primary studies. It is not Hailing Jin's voice and does not claim her present personal opinion. Multi-author papers document team findings; `senior` or corresponding authorship does not turn every sentence into an individual endorsement.

Mechanism evidence is limited to publications available by the project cutoff **2026-07-17**. Retrieval occurred on 2026-07-18. No raw sequencing data were downloaded or reprocessed.

The analysis keeps the following separate:

- donor organism versus recipient organism;
- endogenous sRNA, engineered HIGS RNA, externally applied SIGS RNA and transferred mRNA;
- miRNA family, MIR locus, precursor, mature arm, exact sequence and genomic mapping;
- detection, extracellular release, transport, uptake, effector engagement, target regulation and phenotype causality;
- EV association, intravesicular cargo and non-EV extracellular RNA;
- direct target evidence and complete disease-phenotype causality.

## 1. Default assumptions and null hypotheses

### RDNA-HJ-01 — Foreign reads are not transfer until carryover alternatives are excluded

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C001`, `HJ-B-C003`.

The default null is that a donor-matching RNA signal in infected tissue or a recipient-associated fraction may arise from mixed cells, surface adherence, sample lysis, extracellular carryover, index contamination, shared sequence or ambiguous mapping. Jin-team studies repeatedly added controls that move beyond a mixed-tissue read count: purified interacting fungal cells, cultured pathogen mixed with uninfected leaves, nuclease treatment, vesicle purification, fluorescent localization, recipient effector association and route genetics [HJ-A-S006, HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S014, HJ-A-S015].

Independent extracellular-RNA work reinforces the need for this null. Arabidopsis leaf surfaces, apoplast and operational EV fractions can contain different RNA populations, and abundant extracellular RNA can be non-vesicular [HJ-B-S006, HJ-B-S015]. The linked formal correction changes only the funding acknowledgment and not those compartment findings [HJ-CORR-S002].

**Transferable rule:** begin with contamination, compartment mixing and mapping ambiguity as live alternatives. A cross-kingdom claim is upgraded only by source-specific localization, recipient uptake and molecular activity.

**Failure condition:** if only mixed-tissue sequencing or qPCR is available, the appropriate conclusion is “donor-matching RNA detected in a mixed sample,” not “transported into recipient cells.”

### RDNA-HJ-02 — Cross-kingdom regulation is a chain, not a single observation

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C002`, `HJ-C-C004`.

A recurring decomposition is:

```text
donor production
→ extracellular release
→ inter-organism transport
→ recipient uptake
→ recipient effector engagement or translation
→ direct target effect
→ infection phenotype
```

Botrytis-to-plant work tested fungal sRNA production, host AGO1 association, target repression and virulence genetics [HJ-A-S006], with later work addressing fungal EV release and plant clathrin-mediated uptake [HJ-A-S014]. Plant-to-Botrytis work tested EV association, fungal uptake, fungal target effects and disease [HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S010]. The cotton-Verticillium study used a different plant-pathogen context and target-site-resistant fungal alleles [HJ-B-S012]. The plant-mRNA study added full-length cargo, fungal ribosome/polysome association, translation and host/fungal genetic effects [HJ-A-S015].

**Transferable rule:** attach each assay to exactly one or a few links and report the highest completed link. Do not let evidence at the release or uptake step silently fill the target or phenotype steps.

### RDNA-HJ-03 — Vehicle identity is a graded claim

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C006`, `HJ-B-C007`, `HJ-B-C008`, `HJ-C-C013`, `HJ-C-C014`.

The EV null is not “all extracellular RNA is vesicular.” A useful evidence ladder is:

1. RNA co-pellets with an operational EV fraction;
2. RNA is protected from nuclease and released after membrane disruption;
3. RNA co-fractionates with validated vesicle markers on a density gradient;
4. RNA is enriched in marker-immunoisolated vesicles;
5. secretion/biogenesis perturbation changes extracellular cargo;
6. recipient uptake of labeled or marker-defined vesicles is shown;
7. route perturbation changes recipient molecular action;
8. molecular rescue or infection phenotype follows.

Jin-team work spans several rungs using TET8/TET9, density fractionation, immunoisolation, RBPs, BcPLS1 and clathrin-pathway perturbations [HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S010, HJ-B-S009, HJ-A-S014, HJ-A-S015]. Independent work shows both EV-associated tiny RNAs and substantial non-EV extracellular RNA, which prevents co-pelleting from becoming a universal vehicle claim [HJ-B-S006, HJ-B-S015].

**Transferable rule:** state the operational fraction, marker, protection assay and route perturbation. “EV-mediated” should not be assigned from ultracentrifugation alone.

## 2. Evidence thresholds

### Detection threshold

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C001`, `HJ-C-C004`.

At minimum, report exact sequence or RNA-class level, donor and recipient assemblies, mapping uniqueness, abundance threshold, replicates and compartment. Same family name is not enough to assign the exact molecule or source.

### Transport threshold

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C002`, `HJ-C-C004`.

Require localization inside recipient cells or a comparably discriminating recipient-purification assay plus carryover controls. Surface association, extracellular detection or RNase resistance alone is insufficient [HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S014, HJ-A-S015].

### EV-cargo threshold

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C006`, `HJ-B-C007`, `HJ-B-C008`, `HJ-C-C013`, `HJ-C-C014`.

Require at least marker-defined fractionation plus a protection/topology test; stronger claims add immunoisolation and genetic route perturbation [HJ-A-S010, HJ-B-S009]. EV heterogeneity and non-EV RNA alternatives remain explicit [HJ-B-S006, HJ-B-S015].

### Recipient-action threshold

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C002`, `HJ-B-C014`.

Require a direct recipient event appropriate to RNA class:

- sRNA: recipient AGO association, target cleavage/repression, reporter or target-site perturbation;
- dsRNA: uptake, recipient Dicer processing, sequence-specific target change;
- mRNA: full-length transcript in recipient, ribosome/polysome association and protein production.

The sRNA and mRNA evidence classes must not be collapsed [HJ-A-S006, HJ-A-S008, HJ-A-S014, HJ-A-S015].

### Phenotype-causality threshold

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C011`, `HJ-C-C008`.

Separate three axes:

1. RNA origin/transport;
2. direct target action;
3. phenotype causality.

A strong phenotype claim uses target-site-resistant alleles, recipient machinery mutants, rescue/complementation or equivalent discriminating genetics. Cotton miR166/miR159 studies used fungal target variants resistant to the respective miRNAs [HJ-B-S012]. Botrytis studies used fungal DCL, host AGO1, EV-route and uptake-route perturbations [HJ-A-S006, HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S014].

**Boundary:** direct target evidence is not proof that the target is the sole cause of the full disease phenotype.

## 3. Preferred triangulation

### Triangulation pattern A — Physical + genetic + functional

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C002`, `HJ-C-C005`.

For an RNA transport mechanism, the strongest recurring design combines:

- physical evidence: fractionation, protection, localization, immunocapture;
- genetic evidence: donor production, release route, recipient uptake or effector mutants;
- functional evidence: target change and infection phenotype.

This combination appears across plant-to-fungus EV work and fungus-to-plant EV/CME work [HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S010, HJ-A-S014].

### Triangulation pattern B — Closest alternative-route comparison

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C013`, `HJ-C-C015`, `HJ-C-C016`.

The 2017 SIGS perspective explicitly presented two possible routes: plant entry/passage before fungal uptake versus direct fungal uptake [HJ-B-S003]. Independent Fusarium work demonstrated plant passage in one system [HJ-B-S013], whereas Botrytis work showed direct fungal uptake of external RNA under tested conditions [HJ-A-S008]. These are not contradictory universal answers; they identify a required route test.

### Triangulation pattern C — Recipient machinery before application claims

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C012`, `HJ-C-C015`, `HJ-C-C016`.

Engineered RNA efficacy is most interpretable when pathogen uptake, Dicer processing, target knockdown, lesion/biomass and persistence are measured. Botrytis and Fusarium studies show that these steps and their duration vary [HJ-A-S008, HJ-B-S013, HJ-B-S014].

### Triangulation pattern D — Same mechanism in different biological contexts

**Claim closure:** `HJ-B-C005`, `HJ-C-C008`.

Natural plant-to-fungus regulation appears in Arabidopsis-Botrytis and cotton-Verticillium, but with different RNAs, targets and demonstrated vehicles [HJ-A-S009, HJ-B-S012]. This supports the broad phenomenon while preserving system-specific routes.

## 4. False-positive defenses

### FP-01 — Mixed-sample and host-cell contamination

Use recipient-cell purification and a deliberately mixed control processed through the same purification. Add donor and recipient marker assays. If residual donor cellular markers track the RNA, transport remains unresolved [HJ-A-S009, HJ-A-S015].

### FP-02 — Surface-bound RNA mistaken for uptake

Use extensive washing, nuclease/protease treatment appropriate to topology, membrane-disruption controls and internal fluorescence or recipient biochemical readouts. Uptake is not established by persistence after a weak wash [HJ-A-S008, HJ-A-S014].

### FP-03 — Ultracentrifuge pellet mistaken for a pure EV population

Report the centrifugation sequence, density fraction, EV markers, contaminant markers and immunoisolation results. Plant EV subclasses differ, and RNA composition changes with preparation [HJ-B-S006, HJ-A-S010, HJ-B-S009].

### FP-04 — Ambiguous organismal origin

Require exact sequence and both genomes. Report multi-hit reads, identical conserved sequences, low-complexity filters and assembly versions. Unique mapping can support origin but cannot by itself support transport.

### FP-05 — Predicted target mistaken for direct regulation

Require recipient AGO/effector engagement, cleavage or reporter/target-site evidence. Expression anticorrelation is at most indirect. For phenotype, add resistant-target or rescue genetics [HJ-A-S006, HJ-B-S012].

### FP-06 — Route-mutant pleiotropy

EV and clathrin components have functions beyond RNA traffic. Therefore, route-mutant disease phenotypes must be accompanied by vesicle/cargo, uptake, AGO-loading or target intermediates [HJ-A-S010, HJ-A-S014].

### FP-07 — Engineered efficacy mistaken for natural communication

HIGS and SIGS show that RNA can be used to alter a pathogen. They do not prove that the same sequence is naturally produced or transported by the same route [HJ-A-S008, HJ-B-S003, HJ-B-S013].

### FP-08 — Extracellular RNA abundance mistaken for functional cargo

EV preparations can be enriched in 10–17 nt tiny RNAs of uncertain function, and leaf surfaces can carry abundant non-EV RNAs [HJ-B-S006, HJ-B-S015]. Abundance needs a direct recipient action assay.

## 5. False-negative awareness

- Aggressive RNase treatment can destroy vesicles or accessible RNA-protein complexes and create a route-specific false negative.
- A marker-negative EV subclass may be missed by TET8 or BcPLS1 immunoisolation.
- Low-abundance but functional exact sequences can disappear under bulk compositional summaries.
- Recipient uptake may be infection-stage, tissue, surface or formulation dependent.
- A pathogen lacking sustained secondary amplification can show transient activity that a late time point misses [HJ-B-S014].
- Failure to see RNA in a bulk recipient fraction does not exclude rare-cell uptake; however, rare-cell claims still require localization and function.

## 6. Transfer boundaries

### Species and pathogen boundary

Botrytis, Verticillium and Fusarium differ in cell wall, uptake, RNAi machinery and infection biology. A route or durable effect in one cannot be presumed in another [HJ-A-S008, HJ-B-S012, HJ-B-S013, HJ-B-S014].

### Tissue and infection-stage boundary

Detached leaves, fruits, vegetables and petals are not equivalent to intact field crops. Infection-site EV release can differ from steady-state apoplastic or leaf-surface RNA [HJ-A-S008, HJ-B-S015].

### RNA-class boundary

Endogenous miRNAs, siRNAs, long dsRNAs and full-length mRNAs differ in biogenesis, protection, recipient machinery and readout. The 2024 mRNA evidence is not evidence that every transferred RNA acts by RNAi [HJ-A-S015].

### Vehicle boundary

EV evidence in Arabidopsis-Botrytis does not imply that cotton miR159/miR166 transfer is EV-mediated; S012 did not establish that route. Non-EV extracellular RNA must remain an alternative [HJ-B-S012, HJ-B-S015].

### Application boundary

Spray protection requires formulation stability, environmental persistence, target conservation, off-target evaluation, pathogen uptake and field-scale replication. Short laboratory protection is not a release recommendation [HJ-A-S008, HJ-B-S013, HJ-B-S014].

## 7. Uncertainty language

Use formulations tied to the completed evidence link:

- “Donor-matching reads were detected in the mixed infection sample; transfer is unresolved.”
- “The RNA was enriched in a marker-defined EV fraction and nuclease-protected; recipient uptake was not tested.”
- “Recipient uptake and target repression were observed in this pathosystem; the vehicle remains uncertain.”
- “These data support EV/CME-mediated uptake as a major route in the tested Botrytis-Arabidopsis interaction.”
- “Target-site genetics support sequence-specific contribution to virulence; complete phenotype causality may involve additional targets.”
- “The result is analogous across systems, not yet demonstrated as a conserved route.”
- “This is an Agent inference from published team designs, not a statement by Hailing Jin.”

Avoid:

- “cross-kingdom RNA is proven” without naming direction, RNA, vehicle and causal step;
- “exosome” when only a centrifugation pellet is available;
- “miR166” when family, locus, arm and exact sequence are unresolved;
- “field-ready RNA fungicide” based on detached-tissue assays;
- “Hailing Jin would say” or any first-person impersonation.

## 9. Generic principles to exclude from expert-model status

The following are scientifically reasonable but too generic unless made specific by the models above:

- “Use controls.”
- “Replication is important.”
- “Correlation is not causation.”
- “Extracellular vesicles carry cargo.”
- “Small RNAs regulate genes.”
- “RNAi may protect crops.”

The distinctive content lies in which alternatives are prioritized, how the donor-to-recipient chain is decomposed, how EV evidence is tiered, and which recipient/target perturbations upgrade causality.

## 10. Honest limitations

- Most high-resolution mechanistic evidence comes from the Jin team and the Arabidopsis-Botrytis system; independent studies are essential for scope control.
- Independent sources here provide cross-system support and compartment/application boundaries but are not exact replications of every EV/CME claim.
- Several authorship records document team work; this analysis cannot assign every design choice exclusively to Hailing Jin.
- The literature contains active debates about RNA vehicle, abundance and transport. The correct output is a graded, system-specific assessment, not a binary vote.
- Published sequencing evidence was read as reported; no raw data were downloaded, remapped or independently reanalyzed.
