## Methodological tensions

### Tension T1 — Shared suppressor phenotype versus molecular plurality

**Position A:** Standing reporter and infection studies support viral counterdefense and show that multiple viral proteins suppress PTGS (`claim-carrington-a-001`, `claim-carrington-c-007`).  
**Position B:** Structural and comparative assays show different RNA-binding behaviors, and a common suppressor label does not identify the same molecular step (`claim-carrington-c-010`, `claim-carrington-c-011`).  
**Resolution:** Preserve the functional class, then test small-RNA production, duplex binding, AGO loading, slicing, amplification/spread, native-infection necessity, and symptoms separately. A p19 mechanism is not transferred to HC-Pro or 2b.

### Tension T2 — Primary component assignment versus conditional backup

**Position A:** DCL4 is the principal canonical tasiRNA processor and selected DCL/RDR/AGO components dominate specific tested outputs (`JCC-B-C002`, `claim-carrington-a-007`, `claim-carrington-a-010`).  
**Position B:** Higher-order mutants reveal alternative-sized products, redundancy, and tissue- or virus-conditioned routes (`claim-carrington-c-013`, `claim-carrington-c-017`).  
**Resolution:** Report primary wild-type role separately from mutant-state compensation. Require product size/register, loading/action, and phenotype before calling functional backup.

### Tension T3 — AGO1 specialization versus AGO2 antiviral layering

**Position A:** AGO1 directly loads selected miRNAs/tasiRNAs and is a Slicer, while tested viral siRNAs were not recovered in one 2005 assay (`claim-carrington-c-016`).  
**Position B:** AGO2 genetics and bound vsiRNAs support antiviral roles against TCV/CMV, but not a comparable TMV effect in that study (`claim-carrington-c-012`).  
**Resolution:** Treat this as virus/suppressor/tissue/time/AGO-layer dependence, not a vote for one universal AGO. Use matched AGO loading, catalytic mutants, viral fitness, and time courses.

### Tension T4 — Broad RDR6 antiviral activity versus context-conditioned penetrance

**Position A:** Nicotiana RDR6 knockdown increased susceptibility across a tested virus panel (`claim-carrington-c-015`).  
**Position B:** Magnitude depended on temperature and shoot-apex context, and knockdown is not a clean multi-allele null/rescue series.  
**Resolution:** Keep “broad” limited to the tested panel and design host × temperature × tissue × virus × RDR6-allele experiments before transfer.

### Tension T5 — Cleavage/22-nt rules versus trigger-context specificity

**Position A:** Cleavage can set a phase register, and tested 22-nt guides can confer secondary-siRNA triggering competence (`JCC-B-C003`, `JCC-B-C005`).  
**Position B:** Many cleaved targets do not enter the pathway; AGO, one-hit/two-hit architecture, transcript context, and system-specific candidate RDR/DCL dependency impose conditions. RDR6/DCL4 is supported for the landed canonical TAS and engineered 21-nt contexts, not as a universal PHAS default (`JCC-B-C004`, `JCC-B-C006`, `claim-carrington-a-017`).  
**Resolution:** Use the full trigger–AGO–target-site–register–dependency chain. Treat 22 nt as a tested causal variable, not universal sufficiency or miRNA identity proof.

### Tension T6 — Attractive positive models versus scope-reducing negative results

**Position A:** Genome-scale profiles, rescue, and phenotype correlations can nominate coherent mechanisms (`JCC-B-C008`, `JCC-B-C014`).  
**Position B:** Threshold-sensitive discovery, few direct local effects, and failed independent-allele rescue can narrow or reject those mechanisms (`JCC-B-C009`, `JCC-B-C010`, `JCC-B-C012`, `JCC-B-C016`).  
**Resolution:** Predeclare thresholds, use independent alleles and molecular intermediates, report assay power, and allow a controlled negative result to reduce causal scope.

- `src-doi-10-1093-emboj-17-22-6739` is retracted; `src-doi-10-15252-embj-201570030` is the authoritative status notice.
- It is retained only for provenance/status in `claim-carrington-c-008` and must never support HC-Pro, 2b, PTGS suppression, or antiviral RNAi.

## What would resolve the principal open questions

2. Higher-order component genetics plus product size/register, AGO loading, catalytic rescue, and tissue-resolved phenotype.
3. Matched trigger constructs varying one property at a time, with identity, loading, cleavage, phasing, system-appropriate candidate RDR/DCL dependency, and downstream function.
4. Independent allele/rescue designs that monitor transgene expression and molecular intermediates across generations.
5. Crop- and environment-specific validation rather than name-based orthology or model-system transfer.
