## Research-evolution phases

### 1. Viral counterdefense and silencing maintenance

The 1998 and 2000 studies use viral P1/HC-Pro to expose an RNA-silencing counterdefense and distinguish initiation from maintenance-stage effects. The safe inference is methodological: perturb the pathway with a viral factor and track multiple outputs. It is not evidence that one suppressor mechanism applies to all viruses or hosts.

### 2. Diversification of plant small-RNA machinery

From 2004, combinatorial Arabidopsis genetics separates DCL and RDR functions, while subsequent work connects miRNA-guided events to phased siRNA production. This phase moves from the existence of silencing toward component-specific pathway architecture.

### 3. Trigger, AGO and target-context specificity

The 2005–2012 studies progressively resolve how cleavage register, miRNA length, AGO identity, target-site role and catalytic competence condition secondary-siRNA and antiviral outputs. These papers argue against inferring function from sequence complementarity or component presence alone.

### 4. Modular antiviral defense and quantitative phenotyping

The 2010 and 2019 virus studies compare family members and tissues using molecular and phenotypic outputs. This supports a research trajectory toward conditional, quantitative models of defense rather than a single linear pathway.

### 5. Model-to-crop translation

The 2025–2026 publications apply virus-host and gene-regulation questions to diverse plants and cassava engineering. They support a translational expansion, but the precise claims remain limited to the tested alleles, constructs, species and controls.
