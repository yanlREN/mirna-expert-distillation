## 2. Framework evolution

### From genetic requirement to biochemical step localization

The 2002 work established that DCL1 and HEN1 matter for miRNA metabolism and development. The 2005 methylation studies and later independent structural/kinetic work moved the field from genetic requirement toward substrate-level mechanism. The durable lesson is to avoid using a pleiotropic mutant phenotype as the sole locator of a molecular defect.

### From a single developmental target to plural action modes

The 2004 miR172/AP2 result made protein-level measurement essential. Independent 2008 genetics and 2013 reconstitution then supported translational repression beyond the original case and separated it from slicing. The historical claim that translation repression occurs is supported; any claim that it dominates a new target still requires matched RNA and protein evidence.

### From terminal protection to a turnover network

HEN1 methylation and uridylation protection were a foundation, but the 2015 HESO1/URT1 work and 2020 URT1 structure show that tailing and trimming have redundancy, sequentiality, and competing branches. End heterogeneity therefore needs enzyme/genotype/AGO-context resolution rather than a one-enzyme explanation.

### From cell-autonomous action to spatially gated movement

The 2019–2022 record adds cell type, mobility, and AGO loading to the mechanism. This does not supersede cell-autonomous action. It adds a staged evidence chain: source production → movement → recipient loading → direct target effect → phenotype.

## 3. Historical, contested, and status labels

- **Historical but supported:** the 2002 DCL1/HEN1 genetic link and 2004 miR172/AP2 protein-level regulation.
- **Refined, not superseded:** HEN1 protection; later work added HESO1/URT1 redundancy and trimming.
- **Context-dependent:** relative importance of cleavage, mRNA decay, and translational repression.
- **Contested/active boundary:** whether visible D-bodies quantitatively determine processing for each pri-miRNA, and how broadly specific mobility/loading mechanisms transfer across tissues and species.

## 4. Transferable timeline heuristics

1. A genetic requirement is not yet a biochemical step assignment.
2. Measure target RNA and protein before choosing cleavage/decay versus translation as the main mechanism.
3. Interpret HEN1, tailing, trimming, and decay as a coupled network with redundant enzymes.
4. Treat D-body localization, AGO loading, movement, target engagement, and phenotype as separate tests.
5. Preserve species, tissue, stage, genotype, and assay boundaries when carrying a mechanism forward.
