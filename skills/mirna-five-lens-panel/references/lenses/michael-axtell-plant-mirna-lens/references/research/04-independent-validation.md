## 2. Supported claims

### 2.1 Precise processing is more informative than “looks like a hairpin”

- **Finding:** Independent plant tools operationalize both precursor structure and read-derived processing accuracy, consistent with the 2008/2018 standards (`claim-axtell-c-002`; `src-pmid-26542525`, `src-doi-10-1105-tpc-108-064311`, `src-doi-10-1105-tpc-17-00851`).
- **Why it matters:** A plausible fold can arise from siRNA-generating inverted repeats or chance structures. A candidate gains identity support when reads concentrate at a coherent miRNA/miRNA-star duplex with reproducibility.
- **Species/context:** land plants; the miRA benchmark also includes green algae.
- **Directness:** direct method/computational tests, not genetic proof of function.
- **Limitations:** exact tolerances must be predeclared and lineage-aware. “Lineage-aware” cannot be used to rescue arbitrary noisy hairpins.
- **Confidence:** high for the general requirement; medium for universal numeric thresholds.

### 2.2 Database inclusion is not experimental confirmation

- **Finding:** miRBase's own quality work and independent re-curation found that mature sequences, arms and endpoints can disagree with sequencing evidence (`claim-axtell-c-005`; `src-pmid-22303321`, `src-pmid-30423142`).
- **Why it matters:** A database name is a record of deposition/curation state, not a substitute for locus-, assembly-, tissue- and library-matched evidence.
- **Species/context:** the strongest published discrepancy counts here are animal data; the logic transfers, the percentages do not.
- **Directness:** direct reanalysis of deposited sequences against sRNA-seq.
- **Limitations:** this does not mean all miRBase entries are weak, and database versions differ.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 2.3 Plant-specific reannotation exposes material miRBase conflicts

- **Finding:** PmiREN applied a uniform pipeline and manual curation across 88 plants, reporting 244 corrected miRBase entries and 3059 discarded for insufficient support in its comparison (`claim-axtell-c-006`; `src-doi-10.1093-nar-gkz894`).
- **Why it matters:** This independently extends the 2018 false-positive argument into a plant database build and demonstrates that locus coordinates, mature/star placement and evidentiary tier can change under standardized review.
- **Species/context:** 88 plants with available genomes/sRNA-seq in PmiREN v1.0.
- **Directness:** direct database reanalysis; no raw data were downloaded for this project.
- **Limitations:** PmiREN inclusion is also not equivalent to full biological validation; PARE-backed cleavage is separate from phenotype causality.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 2.4 Young/lineage-specific miRNAs need strong identity evidence, not automatic rejection

- **Finding:** The Axtell/Meyers framework states that conservation is not definitive; independent miRA work shows organism-specific/heterogeneous precursor populations can be recovered when processing evidence is considered (`claim-axtell-c-007`, `claim-axtell-c-008`; `src-doi-10-1105-tpc-17-00851`, `src-pmid-26542525`).
- **Why it matters:** Lack of conservation cannot by itself falsify a young miRNA, but novelty increases the need to exclude siRNA, repeat, tRNA/rRNA fragment and degradation alternatives.
- **Directness:** framework plus independent computational/method support.
- **Limitations:** no claim is made that a newly discovered lineage-specific locus is functional merely because a tool predicts it.
- **Confidence:** high for the evidence logic; candidate-specific conclusions require locus data.

## 3. Contested claims and methodological critiques

### 3.1 ShortStack is useful, not an oracle

- **Finding:** ShortStack reports several locus features and performed well in its original datasets (`src-doi-10-1261-rna-035279-112`), but miRkwood reported better performance in its own benchmark and added a quality score (`claim-axtell-c-003`, `claim-axtell-c-004`; `src-pmid-31253093`).
- **Interpretation:** This is a method- and dataset-dependent comparison, not a contradiction of the need for strict criteria. Both results argue for inspecting evidence fields rather than accepting the software label.
- **What would resolve tool disagreement:** blinded benchmark sets with known positives and hard negatives, identical assemblies/libraries, frozen versions/defaults, per-locus error review, and external validation.
- **Confidence:** high that tool outputs are conditional; medium for any across-the-board ranking.

### 3.2 Strict criteria versus lineage-specific precursor complexity

- **Position A:** strict, replicated precision filters reduce the enormous false-positive burden in plant sRNA data (`src-doi-10-1105-tpc-17-00851`).
- **Position B:** fixed precursor assumptions can reduce sensitivity in lineages with heterogeneous or noncanonical processing (`src-pmid-26542525`).
- **Resolution:** preserve the evidence categories but validate lineage-specific thresholds on appropriate positives/negatives; do not simply waive miRNA-star, precision or replication requirements post hoc.
- **Status:** context-dependent tension, not a settled binary dispute.

### 3.3 Tool concordance can be pseudo-independence

- **Finding:** miRScore explicitly notes that concordance with ShortStack partly reflects shared authorship (`claim-axtell-c-012`; `src-pmid-41183249`).
- **Why it matters:** Different software names do not guarantee independent assumptions, training evidence or implementation lineage.
- **Limitation:** miRScore remains a useful current method; it is simply not counted as independent replication of the Axtell framework.
- **Confidence:** high.

## 4. Context-dependent differences

| Issue | Valid transfer | Invalid transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Animal database re-curation | Audit arms/endpoints and do not equate deposition with validation | Apply animal discrepancy percentages or processing distributions directly to plants |
| Green-algal precursor heterogeneity | Use as warning that divergent lineages may need validated parameter adaptation | Treat every noisy plant hairpin as a legitimate noncanonical miRNA |
| AGO association | Support that a small RNA enters an effector complex | Equate enrichment with MIRNA identity, direct target validation, or phenotype causality |
| PARE | Support a cleavage event in the matched system | Infer that cleavage is the only target or causes the full phenotype |
| Family conservation | Support history of a family-level relationship | Infer orthology of every same-named locus, arm or mature sequence |

## 6. Unresolved conflicts and decisive experiments

1. **Sensitivity versus specificity for young loci.** Resolve with blinded, lineage-matched positive and hard-negative sets; independent libraries; DCL/AGO perturbation where feasible; and explicit failure analysis.
2. **Tool disagreement.** Re-run frozen versions on identical assemblies and unmerged biological replicates; compare per-locus read precision, multimapping, repetitiveness and miRNA-star evidence.
3. **Database conflict.** Trace each record to assembly, genomic coordinate, precursor, mature arm/sequence, library and version; do not merge family-level names with locus-level evidence.
4. **Noncanonical processing.** Require a predeclared alternative biogenesis model and orthogonal perturbation/association evidence rather than relaxing criteria after seeing a candidate.
5. **Function of young miRNAs.** Separate identity (I0–I3) from target directness (F0–F4) and phenotype causality; target prediction must not back-prove identity.

## 7. Bottom line for synthesis

The independent literature strongly supports the Axtell-style false-positive defense and database skepticism. It also imposes two important qualifications: no single annotation program is definitive, and precursor/processing rules need lineage-aware validation without becoming ad hoc. Strict independent support is adequate (five external groups), but tool agreement inside the Axtell–Meyers collaboration network must not be presented as independent replication.
